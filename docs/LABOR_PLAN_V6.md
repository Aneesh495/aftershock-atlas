# Occupational work and sector hours protocol

Extend S04 and S08 with two aggregate labor sources. This protocol is registered before comparing outcomes. The survey items, work-hour denominators and mobility device populations remain distinct.

## US occupational and industry reports

Capture all 29 BLS CPS COVID supplement workbooks from May 2020 through September 2022. Use corrected tables 2 and 7. Table 2 reports workers who teleworked because of the pandemic. Table 7 reports current workers who could not work at some point because their employer closed or lost business. Both questions cover the previous four weeks. Use denominators for the specific table, occupation or industry and month. Keep occupation and industry as separate classifications and preserve major-group levels independently of nested labels.

Primary comparisons are within the same published group and measure across September 2020, 2021 and 2022. Identify absolute changes of at least 5 percentage points as descriptively large. Record telework and unable-to-work measures separately. Telework because of the pandemic does not count all remote work. The difference does not measure personal exposure, occupational infection risk or what would have occurred without the pandemic. Record the May 2020 corrections and the January population control updates in the source notes. Do not join to the October 2022 telework instrument.

The published totals and numerator counts are survey estimates rounded to thousands. Retain published percentages and calculate ratio intervals due to rounding as a validation check. Sampling uncertainty is reported as unavailable unless the exact estimate's provider SE or a documented valid variance method is available. Do not calculate binomial SEs from weighted population counts. Preserve workbook symbols, footnotes and missing or unreliable cells.

## European sector hours

Use Eurostat `lfsq_ewhan2` from 2017-Q1 through 2024-Q4. Select employed adults aged 20 to 64 who worked in their main job during the reference week, both sexes and total working time, in the 27 current EU members. Select total NACE and sectors A (agriculture), C (manufacturing), F (construction), G (trade), I (accommodation and food), J (information and communication), P (education) and Q (health and social work). The outcome is actual hours per reference week at work, released quarterly. People absent for the reference week are outside this denominator.

Prespecify calendar-matched Q2 contrasts for 2019 to 2020 and 2022 to 2023, keeping pre- and post-2021 survey methods separate. Compare each sector to the same country's total-hours change as a descriptive sensitivity in each window. Require numeric amounts and complete flags between the endpoints. Withhold comparisons across a declared break or a discontinuity in definition, confidentiality, or publication support. Carry estimated and provisional flags into the table and report an unflagged sensitivity cohort. Use one hour as the descriptive threshold. Keep unweighted cell summaries distinct from employment-weighted estimates. A reduction among workers present cannot estimate absent workers' hours or total labor input. OECD annual hours per worker across all jobs remain in their own population.

Capture the provider JSON-stat cube with explicit dimensions and all missing statuses. Compare two independent decoders or a separate provider encoding before interpreting contrasts. Limit each workbook to 3 MB compressed and 20 MB expanded and the selected Eurostat cube to 8 MB. Verify pins, exact window identities, label roles, numerator and denominator plausibility and changes in flags without filling missing cells.

Sources are the [BLS corrected monthly tables](https://www.bls.gov/cps/covid-may2020-sept2022-data-tables.htm), [CPS COVID question definitions](https://www.bls.gov/cps/effects-of-the-coronavirus-covid-19-pandemic.htm), [BLS reuse policy](https://www.bls.gov/opub/copyright-information.htm), [Eurostat working-hours definitions](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Hours_of_work_and_absences_from_work_-_quarterly_statistics), [EU-LFS method change](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=EU_Labour_Force_Survey_-_new_methodology_from_2021_onwards), [Eurostat observation flags](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/en/data/database) and [Eurostat attribution terms](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/help/copyright-notice).
