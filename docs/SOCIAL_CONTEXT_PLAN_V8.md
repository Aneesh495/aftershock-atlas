# Age-standardized survey comparisons

Estimate national subgroup differences in food insufficiency and screened anxiety or depression using the 2021 Household Pulse public-use files for weeks 33 and 34. Each wave is a distinct cross-section. The mental-health item window changes from seven days to two weeks. The income reference year changes from 2019 to 2020. Show wave results side by side without pooling or estimating change for either measure. Report food insufficiency as the share of adults aged 25 and older who say their household sometimes or often lacked enough food in the previous seven days. The reference unit is adults living in households. The Census publication describes these as households' food circumstances.

Use national adult person weights and all 80 adult person replicate weights. Compare tenure, Census racial and Hispanic origin categories, and before-tax household income separately. Do not cross-classify these groups. Use the non-Hispanic White only, owner-occupied and $50,000 or more groups as within-wave descriptive comparators. Evaluate health symptoms using the four recorded responses and the same scoring rules already used for the survey bridge. Keep item nonresponse, insufficient cells and survey coverage visible.

Use the NCHS U.S. 2000 standard-million distribution, renormalized over ages 25 and older to ages 25–44, 45–64 and 65+. Estimate age as 2021 minus reported birth year. In sensitivity analyses remove the three youngest birth-year cohorts in each age band because the actual birthday may place them outside that band. Use the same standard weights for every group, outcome and replicate. Compute parallel crude estimates over the same complete-case sample and worst-case bounds for missing outcomes within each age band.

Require at least 100 complete records and at least ten positive and ten negative records in each of the three age bands. Suppress unstable standardized proportions with a relative standard error above 0.30, or undefined replicate denominator. Apply the same rules to the age-boundary sensitivity sample. Withhold all estimates that depend on a suppressed cell within that analysis. No public cell should expose an individually identifying field, exact birth year, individual weight or any underlying small stratum count.

Report standardized prevalence as a weighted mean of three age-specific proportions. Calculate 95% intervals using the provider variance factor of 4/80 and covariance across age bands through the shared person replicates. For subgroup contrasts, difference each pair of estimates within the same replicate and apply Benjamini–Hochberg across prespecified comparisons in each wave and outcome. Treat a five-percentage-point difference as practically large. Inspect whether standardization or the birthday sensitivity meaningfully changes the contrast. Age adjustment does not measure differential access, income loss or a causal effect of tenure or race. Treat food insufficiency as measured material context rather than an individual health risk model.

Benchmark the national unrestricted food-insufficiency complete-case estimate against the corresponding public Census food table when population and item-response denominators match. A positive benchmark outcome requires absolute agreement within 0.1 percentage points. Preserve comparison failures and denominators.
