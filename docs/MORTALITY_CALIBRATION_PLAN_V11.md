# Week-specific mortality forecasting across frozen releases

Use four SHA-identified World Mortality Dataset commits. Use the July 2021 commit as the only feature and model-development archive. The August 2023 commit supplies 2022 calibration outcomes. The May 2025 commit supplies 2024 test outcomes. The September 2026 commit supplies a second observation vintage for the same 2024 targets. Dates refer to the dataset publisher's Git snapshots. They are availability bounds for this harmonized archive.

Freeze weekly all-cause counts for 23 named European countries. Include only explicitly weekly series and weeks 1 through 52. Keep dates and locations as classified by the publisher and do not treat these observations as daily death data. An outcome cohort needs all 52 weeks, without filling missing values or adjusting for residence. Keep visible any country-year missing the complete series. Division by an independently observed early baseline mean provides a scale for the loss function. It does not create a population rate.

Use 2015 to estimate one fixed national count scale and 2016–2018 to estimate a transferable seasonal trend. Use 2019 for validation. Assign one fifth of countries to geographic holdout by the existing SHA-256 rule. No held-out country contributes to ridge coefficients, tuning or calibration. All countries' own pre-2020 histories can set the fixed baseline scale and local comparators, and that conditioning remains explicit. Fit an original pooled ridge model using annual trend and paired annual sine/cosine harmonics at cycles one through three. Freeze penalties 0.1, 1 and 10. Compare 2018 same-week counts, the 2015–2018 same-week mean, and a seasonally damped linear trend. Select ridge penalty and strongest baseline by 2019 normalized MAE. No 2022 or 2024 labels enter fitting.

Forecast the mean with information from the July 2021 release as of the start of 2022 or 2024. Calibrate 90% bands using 2022 weekly absolute residuals of training regions, scaled by each fixed 2015 weekly mean. Use the split-conformal finite-sample rank at the pooled observation count and descriptively report dependence within countries and regime drift across years. Require empirical coverage between 80% and 99% for a practical pass on both the complete cohort and the geographic holdout. Publish the result whatever the outcome. Report interval score, relative count MAE, interval width, separate country and held-out summaries, and a country-cluster bootstrap interval for coverage. Keep May 2025 and September 2026 2024 targets separate for revision sensitivity.

This evaluates forecasting all-cause count uncertainty after a pandemic-era calibration period. It does not measure causes of death, individual risk, or the mortality that would have occurred under a different policy.
