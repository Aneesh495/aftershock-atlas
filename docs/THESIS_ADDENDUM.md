# Evidence update

This addendum records the November 20 research extensions. Earlier paper chapters preserve their original cohorts, dates and failures. Run-level receipts distinguish local independent calculations, hosted source replay, and browser deployment. Working analyses cover specific countries, outcomes and intervals. Aggregate evidence cannot identify individual risks.

## Economic, educational and health reporting

The OECD analysis compares annual actual hours per employed person. The Eurostat analysis compares weekly hours in the main job among workers who were present at work, by sector and country. These populations are distinct. In the 2020 second-quarter comparison, median weekly hours in accommodation and food services fell by about 3.05 hours across 26 eligible European Union countries. The median for all activities was about 0.75 hours. Series breaks remain visible and are not crossed by the reported contrast. The three September Current Population Survey snapshots use person weights and separate pandemic telework from inability to work. All three national telework benchmarks aligned within 0.07 percentage points of the corresponding Bureau of Labor Statistics results.

PISA trends retain the published change uncertainty and assessment coverage flags. They measure eligible enrolled students. The Household Pulse adult internet-based instruction measure has a separate sample and denominator. Its percentages cannot be read as counts or proportions of students.

Hospital admissions, ICU utilization, PCR testing and vaccination data preserve separate counting units and report dates. The state-week surveillance replay checks nearly 30,000 rows across 51 jurisdictions. ICU use uses matched staff and occupied-bed denominators. PCR positivity uses determinate test results and preserves late corrections. Vaccination combines CDC resident series according to their recorded report basis. Raw survey respondents do not enter public data or browser exports.

## Age, tenure and material context

Two independent 2021 Household Pulse cross-sections use all 80 person replicates. Both food-insufficiency benchmarks match the Census national tables. Direct standardization over ages 25 and older produces 88 separate primary and sensitivity estimates, four withheld under the disclosure rule. Renters report food insufficiency 11.60 percentage points more often than owners in week 33 and 10.28 points more often in week 34. Both comparisons retain late-calendar age sensitivity and complete-case item bounds.

Separate source categories identify age, race and Hispanic origin, and before-tax household income. They are descriptive. The income reference year and symptom recall period change between waves, and the weighted household response rates fall near 6%. The analysis does not estimate a causal effect of tenure or treat these waves as matched adults.

## Release dates and administrative boundaries

The UK real GDP archive includes 36 dated releases for 18 quarters. Each growth rate compares levels in the same edition. Two quarter revisions reached 0.25 percentage points. The 2022 second-quarter estimate moved from a small contraction to growth. The verified comparison uses release dates from dated ONS bulletins and archive identities from actual workbook bytes. It disregards unrelated monthly reports when forming the declared quarterly information set.

Connecticut's 2020 resident population of 3,605,944 follows 169 complete municipalities into nine planning regions. The geographic crosswalk reconciles to all original eight county populations and all nine new published totals. Seven historic counties split across multiple regions. This is a count-conserving resident allocation. Mortality by place of occurrence requires a separately matched geography.

The UN M49 audit checks 1,029 coverage cells across seven successor economies and six WDI archives plus the current panel. Of 7,350 source rows, 1,961 populated observations are dated before or during a statistical territory change. Modern identifiers cannot establish the method used for a historical backcast. The WDI archived release bounds do not provide country-by-indicator day-level publication histories or complete historical geography.

## Dated forecast tests

UK GDP models predict first-published growth from lagged quarters in the preceding national accounts archive. Nine earlier quarters calibrate prediction intervals. In eight 2024-25 quarters, forecasts from the validation-selected ridge have an MAE of 0.295 percentage points and cover eight first estimates. The selected zero baseline has an MAE of 0.329. The four-quarter mean has a lower MAE than either selected model, with a wider interval. The eight-of-eight coverage result has a wide exact binomial interval.

The all-cause weekly mortality test uses four explicit archive versions. Early data establish features, a separate pandemic year provides residual calibration, and 2024 outcomes are held out. Twenty-two countries and 1,144 unique weeks support the primary comparison. Czechia, Italy and the Netherlands are excluded from fitting, validation and residual calibration. The ridge's observed coverage is 84.9% overall and 89.1% in the geographic holdout. The seasonal mean makes more accurate point forecasts. Its intervals are wider and its held-out coverage exceeds 99%. The original annual mortality and life expectancy undercoverage remains in the historical charts.
